Method

The method behind the record.

ESG data is only useful when you can see where it came from, understand how it was treated, and identify what is missing.

Search the record About the Foundation

The rule is simple.

If the data exists, show the source. If the data is estimated, say so. If the data is disputed, say so. If the data does not exist, publish the gap.

01 / Purpose

Build an open record, not another black box.

ESG Foundation is a non-profit knowledge base for environmental, social and governance data. We aggregate disclosures, filings and public reporting from across the ESG field, publish the method we use to do it, and make the record free to read.

The Foundation is designed around evidence and provenance. A number without a source is not a useful public record. A missing number should not be silently filled with an assumption.

Our method therefore puts the source, date, treatment and status of the information alongside the record wherever possible.

In practice

RECORD PRINCIPLE

Source first.
Every published figure should be traceable to the evidence used to record it.

02 / Principles

Four principles govern the record.

01

Evidence over assertion

We distinguish disclosed information from claims, commentary and unsupported statements. Company statements remain attributable to the company that made them.

02

Provenance is part of the data

Source, publication date and relevant identifiers are retained with the record so that users can understand where information came from.

03

Gaps remain visible

Where an organisation does not disclose a relevant indicator, the record can show no data rather than inventing completeness.

04

Corrections stay possible

ESG records change. New disclosures, corrections and evidence can change what is recorded. The method therefore supports review and correction rather than treating a published record as permanently fixed.

05

Comparable does not mean identical

Definitions, reporting boundaries, periods and methodologies can differ between organisations. Those differences should be visible before numbers are compared.

06

Method before judgement

The Foundation publishes the evidence and treatment used to construct the record. It does not sell a score as a substitute for the underlying evidence.

03 / Evidence

What counts as evidence?

The record can contain information from different public sources. The source type matters, and so does the context in which a statement was made.

  • Regulatory filings
    Formal filings and regulatory disclosures are treated as primary evidence for the information they contain.
  • Company disclosures
    Annual reports, sustainability reports, ESG disclosures and other published company materials may provide evidence of reported performance, policy or commitments.
  • Official sources
    Government departments, regulators, standards bodies and other official sources may provide relevant data, definitions, requirements and contextual evidence.
  • Research
    Academic research, institutional research and published studies may provide contextual or comparative evidence. Their methods and limitations remain relevant to interpretation.
  • Public reporting
    Reputable public reporting may be recorded as a source of information or context. Reporting is not automatically treated as equivalent to a primary disclosure.
  • Company claims
    Claims made by an organisation are identified as claims. They are not silently converted into independently verified facts.

04 / Record status

Every data point needs a status.

Status tells the reader how the information should be understood. It prevents an estimated or disputed figure from appearing identical to a directly sourced disclosure.

Status Meaning What the reader should understand
Verified source The recorded information is supported by an identified source and has been checked against the applicable evidence. The source is available for inspection.
Estimated The figure is an estimate rather than a directly disclosed value. The treatment should be identifiable in the record. Do not treat the figure as a directly reported disclosure.
Disputed Relevant evidence or claims conflict, have been challenged, withdrawn or otherwise require qualification. The disagreement or qualification should remain visible.
No data No suitable public disclosure has been identified for the relevant indicator and reporting period. Absence of data is itself part of the public record.

05 / Process

From source to published record.

The Foundation's process is designed to preserve provenance while making a large public record practical to maintain.

  1. Identify

    Relevant disclosures, filings, research, official sources and public reporting are identified for the subject and indicator being recorded.

  2. Classify

    The information is classified by source type, topic, geography, reporting period and relevance to the ESG record.

  3. Check

    The source and relevant evidence are reviewed for consistency, date, scope, definition and other factors that could change how the information should be interpreted.

  4. Record

    The information is entered with its source, date, status and relevant contextual information rather than as an isolated number.

  5. Review

    Information requiring additional judgement, qualification or correction is subject to human review before it is treated as part of the published record.

  6. Publish

    The public record presents the available evidence, its status and relevant source information. Where suitable evidence is absent, the gap remains visible.

06 / AI and automation

Machines can process the record. They do not replace the method.

The Foundation is designed to be machine-readable and capable of processing information at scale. Automated systems can help identify sources, classify material, detect duplicates, extract relevant information and flag records for review.

Automation does not turn an unsupported statement into evidence. It does not remove the need for provenance, status or human review where interpretation is required.

The objective is a record that machines can query and people can inspect.

Machine-first does not mean machine-only.

Automation helps us process information. The published record still needs evidence, provenance and visible uncertainty.

07 / Missing data

We do not fill the gaps to make the record look complete.

Missing disclosure is common across ESG reporting. The method treats absence of suitable public evidence differently from a measured zero.

No disclosure

No suitable public source has been identified for the relevant indicator and period. The record shows no data.

Zero

A zero should only be recorded when the underlying source actually reports or supports a zero value. Zero is not the same as missing.

Estimate

Where an estimate is used, it must be identified as an estimate. Estimated information is not presented as directly disclosed data.

Conflicting evidence

Where relevant sources conflict, the conflict should be recorded and qualified rather than silently selecting the more convenient figure.

08 / Comparability

A number is not comparable simply because it has the same label.

ESG indicators can differ in definition, organisational boundary, geography, reporting period, methodology and assurance. Those differences matter.

Before comparing records, users should consider whether the underlying definitions and reporting boundaries are sufficiently aligned for the comparison being made.

The Foundation's role is to make the underlying record visible. Users remain responsible for the conclusions they draw from it.

09 / Corrections

The record can change when the evidence changes.

A public data record should be correctable. New disclosures, corrected filings, withdrawn statements or better source evidence may require a record to be updated.

New evidence
A newly published primary source may update or add to a record.
Correction
Where a source corrects previously published information, the record should reflect the corrected evidence.
Withdrawal
Withdrawn or disputed information should not remain presented without the relevant qualification.
Method and record changes should remain distinguishable.

A change to the Foundation's methodology is different from a change to an individual record because new evidence has appeared.

10 / Scope

What the Foundation is — and what it is not.

ESG Foundation is an open knowledge base and public record for ESG information.

We aggregate information, document sources, publish our method and make the public record free to read.

The Foundation is not a certifier, campaigner, seal or substitute for a regulator, auditor, ratings agency or professional adviser.

The public record is intended to make evidence easier to find, inspect and understand — not to remove the need for professional judgement where it is required.

Open record.

The method is public because the way data is assembled matters as much as the data itself.

Method statement

The record should show what is known — and what is not.

That is the standard we are building towards: source-linked data, visible treatment, explicit uncertainty and no manufactured completeness.
