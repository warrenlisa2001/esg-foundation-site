UAE Formalises ESG Disclosure Metrics as Sustainable Bond Issuance Hits a Record AED 28.6 Billion

August 28, 2026 · ESG Foundation

Abu Dhabi Corniche skyline. Photo: Karthik B K / Unsplash.

The Abu Dhabi Securities Exchange published ESG Disclosure Guidance for listed companies in June 2025, standardising reporting around roughly 40 metrics — 12 environmental, 13 social, 11 governance, and 3 integrated KPIs — aligned to IFRS S1/S2 and GRI. The Securities and Commodities Authority now mandates ESG and sustainability disclosure for listed companies across the market.

The push toward standardised governance disclosure has coincided with record sustainable-finance activity. UAE entities issued AED 28.6 billion in sustainable bonds and sukuk during 2025, up 6% year-on-year and equal to roughly 31.7% of total sustainable issuance across the MENA and Turkey region. Regionally, sustainable sukuk issuance across the Middle East hit a record $11.4 billion in 2025, with the UAE and Saudi Arabia together accounting for an estimated 70–80% of Middle East sustainable bond issuance.

“Governance disclosure tends to move first because it's the cheapest to standardise — naming a board committee costs a company far less to report accurately than an audited Scope 3 figure. The metrics guidance closes some of that gap, but it doesn't close all of it,” says Ivano Iannelli, Chairman of ESG Foundation.

Chairman, ESG Foundation

Chairman of ESG Foundation and CEO of TRST01, Ivano Iannelli brings two decades connecting sustainability economics to commercial performance — including as former sustainability lead on a USD 250 million decarbonisation fund.
